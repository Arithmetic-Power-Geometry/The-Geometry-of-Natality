# GoN prior-art falsification: emerging rule types

The following existing literature materially limits GoN novelty claims:

- Fernández, Mackie and Pinto, **A Higher-Order Calculus for Graph Transformation**, ENTCS, DOI 10.1016/j.entcs.2002.09.005: higher-order graph rewriting already defined.
- Fernández and Gabbay, **Nominal Rewriting with Name Generation: Abstraction vs. Locality**, PPDP 2005: fresh-name creation, alpha-conversion and local state already formalized. https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/nominal-rewriting-with-name-generationabstraction-vs-locality/
- Pira, **A novel approach to solve AI planning problems in graph transformations**, Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence 92 (2020) 103684, DOI 10.1016/j.engappai.2020.103684: planning directly in graph transformation systems already benchmarked.
- **A Logic for Fresh Labelled Transition Systems**, CSL 2026, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.23: formal models of history-sensitive fresh-data generation and model checking.
- **Graded Semantics of Nominal Systems**, CONCUR 2026, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.CONCUR.2026.47: global/local freshness semantics and trace equivalence.

Evidence checked from publishers and author/institutional pages on 2026-10-18. These references are specific obstacles, not a systematic search or complete BibTeX audit.

**Outcome:** the proposed novelty premise 'new action types exist, therefore GoN is new' is not supported. The finite-horizon compilation theorem gives an additional direct mathematical obstruction. The next research question must specify resource bounds, restricted semantics and a competitive baseline before claiming novelty.
