# The Geometry of Natality (GoN): the beginning, not merely the birth of a vertex

Research position (2026-10-18): conceptual reset; no new numbered version and no claim of Q1 readiness.

## Philosophical source
Hannah Arendt, *The Human Condition* (1958), p. 246 (edition-dependent pagination):
“Men, though they must die, are not born in order to die but in order to begin.”
The quotation concerns action and the possibility of beginning something new; it is not a mathematical axiom. See Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/arendt/index.html ; Cambridge discussion https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/politics-in-dark-times/arendts-augustine/E1BC7C8DC9CE4288702800CE8897B618 .

## Corrective distinction
1. **Arrival**: a new vertex or identifier appears. Previous GoN models mainly formalize this.
2. **Activation**: a previously unreachable state becomes reachable. This is classical reachability.
3. **Initiation**: an agent's action changes the future *space of available actions or possible histories* rather than merely choosing a known path.
4. **Human natality**: Arendt's ethical and political concept, not reducible without argument to any of the above.

The poster's straight-line trajectory versus nonlinear intervention is an artistic metaphor. Tangents, vectors and matrices should not be treated as literal scientific descriptions of consciousness.

## Formal candidate: action-space expansion
A rooted labelled transition system has state x and enabled action set A(x). For a history h, let Futures(h) denote the set of observable future action traces admitted after h. An intervention can change Futures(h), but ordinary planning and graph rewriting already do so. Define a proposed research object only after specifying:
- a fixed comparison class of permissible interventions;
- which observations are available to an agent;
- identity semantics and fresh-name generation;
- whether the grammar is fixed or itself rewritable;
- a measurable quantity invariant under state renaming;
- an exact classical baseline.

A larger Futures set is not by itself “natality”; redundant actions and renamed states can inflate it. Require quotienting by observational equivalence and controlling costs and horizon.

## Explicit adversarial test
If the action grammar is fixed and finite, a state-space planner can precompute the reachable transition graph. Then any 'new possibility' is new only to the observer, not to the mathematical transition system. Any stronger GoN claim must either (i) formalize an open-ended grammar with defensible operational semantics, or (ii) show a novel theorem or algorithm for an established class without pretending its state space is unprecedented.

## Closest technical prior art
Nominal logic and fresh-name semantics: Andrew Pitts (2003), DOI 10.1016/S0890-5401(03)00138-X.
Proof-relevant logical relations for name generation: Benton, Hofmann, Nigam (2018), DOI 10.23638/LMCS-14(1:25)2018.
Fresh-register automata and nominal automata also handle new names, finite registers and observational equivalence. These are *obstacles to novelty claims*, not GoN citations of priority.

## Publication decision
Q1 readiness: NO. Philosophy can give a coherent motivating research question, but cannot replace an original theorem, independent comparison or validated empirical contribution.
Stop rule: do not create GoN V16 from this conceptual distinction. First construct a precise falsifiable model and demonstrate a nontrivial difference from established nominal and graph-transformation semantics.
